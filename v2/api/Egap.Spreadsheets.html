Namespace Egap.Spreadsheets

Classes

Cell

A cell of a sheet: a small handle that reads and writes the sheet's store, made by sheet["B4"] or sheet.Cells[row, column]. The sheet keeps no handles; a cell with no value and the default style takes no room. Two handles for the same cell are equal. For bulk work, GetValue(int, int) and SetValue(int, int, CellValue) read and write values without a handle.

CellAlignment

The alignment, wrapping, indent and rotation of a cell's text.

CellBorders

A cell's borders: four edges and the diagonals.

CellFill

The fill behind a cell: a pattern in a foreground colour over a background colour.

CellFont

A cell's font.

CellProtection

What sheet protection does to a cell.

CellStyle

How a cell looks: its font, fill, borders, alignment, protection and number format. A style is an immutable value; change one with with, as in cell.Style = cell.Style with { Font = cell.Style.Font with { Bold = true } }, or for many cells at once with UpdateStyle(Func<CellStyle, CellStyle>). A workbook keeps each distinct style once, as Excel's cell formats table does, however many cells use it.

DefinedName

A defined name: a name for a cell, a range, a constant or a formula, such as TaxRate for Sheet1!$B$1. A name belongs to the workbook, or to one sheet when it is local to that sheet. Excel's own names start with _xlnm., such as _xlnm.Print_Area.

DefinedNameCollection

A workbook's defined names. A name is unique among the workbook's names, or among one sheet's, case ignored.

MergedCellCollection

A sheet's merged cells: ranges shown as one cell, with the value and style of their top-left cell. Merged ranges do not overlap.

PageSetup

How a sheet prints: paper, orientation, scaling, margins, centring, gridlines, headings, page order and the header and footer. The print area and the rows and columns repeated on each page are defined names, _xlnm.Print_Area and _xlnm.Print_Titles, in Names.

SheetColumn

One column of a sheet: its width, whether it is hidden, its outline level and the style of its empty cells.

SheetColumnCollection

A sheet's columns by zero-based index, and, when enumerated, the columns that have a width, are hidden, outlined or styled.

SheetRow

One row of a sheet: its height, whether it is hidden, its outline level and the style of its empty cells.

SheetRowCollection

A sheet's rows by zero-based index, and, when enumerated, the rows that have a height, are hidden, outlined or styled.

SheetView

How a sheet shows on screen: gridlines, headings, zoom, direction, frozen panes and whether its tab is selected.

Workbook

A spreadsheet workbook: its worksheets, defined names, and the text and styles the sheets share. Create one in code, or load an XLSX, XLSM, XLTX or XLTM file and save it again with what it holds kept, the parts this model does not cover included. A workbook is not safe to change from more than one thread at a time.

WorkbookLoadOptions

How a workbook is loaded.

WorkbookSaveOptions

How a workbook is saved.

Worksheet

One sheet of a workbook: up to 1,048,576 rows by 16,384 columns of cells, stored only where cells hold a value or a style. Reach a cell by its A1 address, sheet["B4"], or by zero-based row and column, sheet.Cells[3, 1], and a rectangle of cells with sheet.Range("A1:D20").

WorksheetCells

A sheet's cells: any cell by zero-based row and column, and, when enumerated, the cells that hold a value, a formula or a style, row by row and left to right.

WorksheetCollection

A workbook's worksheets in tab order; names are unique, case ignored, as in Excel.

Structs

CellAddress

Where a cell is on a sheet: a zero-based row and column, written in A1 style as Excel shows it (B4 is row 3, column 1). Rows go up to 1,048,575 and columns up to 16,383 (XFD), the size of an Excel sheet.

CellBorder

One edge of a cell's borders.

CellColor

A colour as spreadsheets give it: automatic, a fixed ARGB colour, a theme colour with a tint, or a legacy palette index. The default value is automatic.

CellRange

A rectangle of cells on a sheet, such as sheet.Range("A1:D20"): a cheap value that reads and writes the sheet's cells. Enumerating it gives every cell in it, empty ones too, row by row; UsedCells gives only the cells that hold something.

CellValue

A cell's value: empty, a number, text, TRUE or FALSE, or an error. Numbers, text, booleans and errors convert to it implicitly, so cell.Value = 42 and cell.Value = "Total" work.

RangeAddress

A rectangle of cells on a sheet, from its top-left cell to its bottom-right cell, written in A1 style as A1:D20. A single cell is written as B4, whole columns as A:C and whole rows as 2:5.
