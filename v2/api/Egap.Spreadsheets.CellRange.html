A rectangle of cells on a sheet, such as sheet.Range("A1:D20"): a cheap value that reads and writes the sheet's cells. Enumerating it gives every cell in it, empty ones too, row by row; UsedCells gives only the cells that hold something.

CopyTo(Cell)

Copies the range's cells to a place on a sheet of the same workbook, as Excel's Copy and Paste do: values, formulas, styles, merges, notes, links, validations and conditional formats. A formula's relative references move with it, and one that would move off the sheet becomes #REF!; absolute ones stay. What was in the cells written over goes, though, as in Excel, a copy onto part of itself keeps the conditional formats of the cells written over and, when it takes no links, their links. A merge the range cuts is copied as the part of it in the range when its first cell is in the range, and a link the range cuts as the part of it in the range. On a sheet whose filter hides rows, only the rows that show are copied, one under another; when they come in more than one run, formulas are pasted as their results.

public void CopyTo(Cell destination)

Parameters

destination Cell

The cell the range's first cell goes to.

Examples

sheet.Range("A1:B3").CopyTo(sheet["D1"]);

Exceptions

ArgumentNullException

The destination is null.

ArgumentException

The destination is in another workbook, or the cells would run past the end of the sheet.

InvalidOperationException

The copy would take part of an array formula, write over part of an array formula or merged cell, cut a merge while going onto its own cells, or write over a pivot table; or it copies rows a filter shows in more than one run onto its own cells.

MoveTo(Cell)

Moves the range's cells to a place on a sheet of the same workbook, as Excel's Cut and Paste do: everything CopyTo(Cell) copies, and tables wholly in the range on the same sheet. Formulas that move keep their text, and every reference in the workbook to the cells that move follows them; a reference to a cell written over becomes #REF!. A range with only an edge in the cells that move, or in the cells written over, changes as in Excel: moved to another sheet, it loses that edge; moved straight along its rows or columns on the sheet, its ends follow the cells that move and pull back from the cells written over. Merges the cells land on are unmerged, as in Excel.

public void MoveTo(Cell destination)

Parameters

destination Cell

The cell the range's first cell goes to.

Examples

sheet.Range("A5:A6").MoveTo(sheet["C40"]);

Remarks

Sheet protection does not block the move: it guards what a person does in Excel, not what code does, as for writing cells.

Exceptions

ArgumentNullException

The destination is null.

ArgumentException

The destination is in another workbook, or the cells would run past the end of the sheet.

InvalidOperationException

The move would take or write over part of an array formula, take part of a merged cell or table, take a table to another sheet, or change a pivot table.

SetArrayFormula(string)

Makes the range an array formula, as Ctrl+Shift+Enter does in Excel: one formula, stored in the range's first cell, that works on arrays and gives each cell of the range one element of its result. A result smaller than the range gives #N/A past its end, and a single row or column is repeated across it. The cells' values are cleared until the workbook is calculated; their styles stay. Setting another formula or value in the first cell ends the array formula.

public void SetArrayFormula(string formula)

Parameters

formula string

The formula, in Excel's stored form; a leading = is dropped.

Examples

sheet.Range("C1:C3").SetArrayFormula("A1:A3*B1:B3");

Exceptions

ArgumentNullException

The formula is null.

ArgumentException

The formula is empty or longer than 8,192 characters.

FormulaException

The formula does not follow Excel's grammar.
